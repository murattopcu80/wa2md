# Language support

The tool uses only the Python standard library. It was written first for Turkish chats; the language support is described honestly in below, and full English support is being built (see [issue #2](https://github.com/murattopcu80/wa2md/issues/2)).

**English is the primary language and Turkish is a supported language** (decision in [issue #1](https://github.com/murattopcu80/wa2md/issues/1); the Turkish README is [README.tr.md](../README.tr.md)). The engine is language-neutral; everything that depends on a language lives in a **language pack** (`wa2md_lang/en.py`, `wa2md_lang/tr.py`; interface and how to add a language: [docs/language-packs.md](language-packs.md); plan and status: [issue #2](https://github.com/murattopcu80/wa2md/issues/2)). State of this branch:

- **Two switches, one rule each.**
  - `--lang en|tr` (or `WA2MD_LANG`, default English) selects the help and console messages **and the labels written into the output**: `Chat-N`, `Person N`, `[phone]`, `[company]`, `[path]`, `[email]`, `[user]`, `[password]`, `[code]`, `[iban]`, `[national-id]`, `[plate]`, `Participants:` / `Message count:` / `Date range:`, the notes (`_(media not included)_`, ...), `media-N.ext`, `attachments.md` and the review file `Chat-N-suspect-names.txt`. `--lang tr` gives the Turkish labels (`Sohbet-N`, `Kişi N`, `[telefon]`, ...) exactly as before.
  - `--packs auto|en|tr|en,tr` (or `WA2MD_PACKS`, default `auto`) selects the packs used to find names, titles, contact words, trade names and secrets **in the chat text**. `auto` decides per chat from the chat's own messages: a pack scores the share of words that are its frequent function words (only words no other common Latin-script language shares) or contain letters only its language uses (Turkish: `ğ ı ş İ`; `ö ü ç` and short words such as `de`, `da`, `mi` are shared with other languages and count for nothing). Every pack that scores at least 4 % and at least 0.35 x the best score is selected; when the best score is below 10 % (a chat in Spanish, French, Dutch, Portuguese, German or another language without a pack, emoji or media only) every pack is selected (over-masking is the safe side). When one language clearly dominates a mixed chat (it wins at least 3/4 of at least 6 messages that carry a signal), a name of the other pack that is an ordinary word of the dominant language is masked only with strong evidence (honorific, suffix or apostrophe, a following surname, a contact field or phone, a participant): `tamam, Kim gelecek` keeps `Kim`, `Kim Hanım geldi` does not. Several packs at once are merged (word lists and patterns united).
  - The export's own words (system lines such as `X added Y`, attachment and media-omitted markers, AM/PM) are always read with every pack, whatever the selection: a Turkish chat exported from a phone set to English (English file name and system lines, Turkish messages) is detected as Turkish by its messages and anonymized with the Turkish rules exactly as before, and an English chat from a Turkish-language phone gets the English rules.
- **English pack (new):** given names (5,888, Wikidata, UK and US, plus 2,513 given names of more origins common among English speakers), surnames (6,732, US Census 2010), 371 English names that are also ordinary words (`mark`, `will`, `rose`, `hope`, `grace`, `bill`, `may`, ...: in lower case and at the start of a sentence they are not masked but written to the review file; in mid-sentence capitals, with a title, with `'s`, or with a surname they are masked), titles before a name (`Mr`, `Mrs`, `Ms`, `Miss`, `Mx`, `Dr`, `Prof`, `Sir`, `Dame`, `Lord`, `Lady`, `Uncle`, `Aunt`, `Coach`, `Officer`, ... with or without the dot; `Dear Sir or Madam` and `Dear all` stay), contact words (`contact`, `call`, `phone`, `tel`, `mobile`, `cell`, `WhatsApp`, ...), trade names before a sector word or a legal form (`Logistics`, `Freight`, `Haulage`, `Removals`, ...; `Ltd`, `Inc`, `LLC`, `LLP`, `PLC`, `Corp`, `GmbH`, `Pty`), business domains (`.com`, `.co.uk`, `.com.au`, ...), password keywords and `is` (`my PIN is 4321`), one-time-code words (`code`, `verification`, `OTP`, `2FA`), UK/Irish IBAN, month and day names (never names), a place list (US states, UK nations, Canadian provinces, Australian states, countries, 104 large cities) and the English attachment labels. A Turkish name that is an ordinary English word (`can`, `hare`, `lee`, `mine`, `said`, ...) is treated as ambiguous whenever the English pack is active, so it is not masked in lower case in English text; a contraction with `'t` (`don't`, `can't`, `ain't`) is never read as a name with a suffix.
- **Language-independent:** sender names from the export (any script) and their occurrences in the text, `@mentions`, people in system lines, e-mail addresses, social/WhatsApp links, international phone numbers that start with `+` or `00`, known secret formats (`ghp_...`, `sk-...`, JWT, long hex/base64, `Bearer ...`).
- **Phones (English pack and every pack):** Turkish mobile numbers (`0532 111 22 33`, `(0532) 111 22 33`, `05321112233`) are masked whatever packs are selected; an IBAN of any country (country code, check digits, mod-97 check) is masked whole as `[iban]` before the phone rule. UK national numbers (`07700 900123`, `020 7946 0123`, `0161 496 0123`, `(0113) 496 0123`, `0800 123 4567`, ungrouped `07700900123`) and US/Canada numbers (`(202) 555-0147`, `202-555-0147`, `202.555.0147`, `1-202-555-0147`) are masked as `[phone]`; `202 555 0147` and ungrouped `2025550147` only right after a phone word (`call`, `tel`, `my number is`, `call me on`, ...); an extension (`ext. 204`, `x204`) is part of the phone. `+<country> ...` and `00 <country> ...` numbers are masked with every pack. Guards: never inside a word or a link, after a decimal point, before a further digit group or a unit (`250 300 1500 kg`), and never dates, times, prices, thousands groupings, versions, coordinates, tracking, card or SSN-like numbers. With the optional `phonenumbers` package (`pip install "wa2md[phone]"`, Apache-2.0) a `+`/`00` candidate whose length is impossible for its country code stays text; without it every candidate is masked; national shapes are always masked.
- **Several packs in one chat (cross-pack rule):** when `auto` picks more than one pack (or `--packs en,tr`), a given name of one pack that is an ordinary word of another selected pack (its `*-common.txt` list) and no given name there is treated as a word-name: 34 English given names are ordinary Turkish words (`ben`, `kim`, `kira`, `ara`, `ana`, ...), 9 Turkish given names are ordinary English words (`can`, `mine`, `said`, `nail`, ...). So lower-case Turkish `ben geldim`, `kim aradı` stay, while `yesterday Ben called` is masked; a participant named Kim is always masked. A lower-case English given name that is an ordinary English word (`300 miles`, `rice`) stays the word in a mixed chat as with English alone.
- **Console:** with `auto` one line per chat names the selected packs, e.g. `Language packs (auto, from the chat text): en - 1 of 2`; an explicit `--packs`/`WA2MD_PACKS` prints nothing.
- **Not yet for English (next steps of issue #2):** US/UK national ids (SSN, NI number) and card numbers are not masked (they stay text, not phones), export layouts with an ISO, year-first or hyphenated date and other languages' system lines, the state folder name `inceleme`.
- **Known limits of the English rules** (same as the Turkish ones): a name in no dictionary without any hint is not masked (`I met Zorvan Quell`, `Ask for Kowalczyk at reception`); an ambiguous name at the start of a sentence stays (`Grace and I will be late`, `Pat and Chris both said yes` keeps `Pat`; it is listed in the review file); a capitalized word right after a capitalized sentence start counts as a title form (`Ask Will about it` keeps `Will`); a place that is also a common given name (`Austin`, `Charlotte`, `Georgia`, `Sydney`, `Victoria`, `Virginia`) is masked like a name. Only in a chat with both packs (the Turkish rules read lower case as a possible name): lower-case words before a capitalized legal form join the trade name (`send it to Quillfield Haulage Ltd` -> `[company] Haulage Ltd`), an unknown surname after a given name is masked only in a surname position (`the new driver, Oskar Lindqvist, starts Monday` keeps `Lindqvist`), and an English name that is an ordinary Turkish word stays at the start of a sentence (`Ben said he would be late`). Over-masking kept on purpose (a capital in mid-sentence is the English name signal): a word-name with a capital in mid-sentence is masked (`and Art supplies`, `the Joy of driving`, `Grace and Hope are virtues`, `Docker and Rust`), a place or brand that is also a given name or surname (`via Paris`) is masked like a name, and a qualifier that is also a listed surname before a legal form becomes a trade name (`Best Ltd` -> `[company] Ltd`). Not found: an initial before an unlisted surname (`J. Zabrowski`), a UK mobile number in a chat that selects only the Turkish pack (the Turkish pack's output stays as before).
